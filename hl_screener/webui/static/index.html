Paper

      
Follow the shortlist live, with no money

      
One virtual account per leader. Every trade the leader makes arrives over Hyperliquid's public feed; the follower's order is priced by walking the real order book at that moment, pays taker fees and hourly funding. Nothing is sent to the exchange. Measured slippage and latency sit next to what the screen's model assumed, which is the point: it tells you whether the screen's numbers were true.

Pump

      
Who snipes pump.fun, who trades it well, and could you copy them

      
Every pump.fun trade on Solana, read from the program's own events over Solana's public RPC, and every PumpSwap trade of those tokens once they graduate. Only tokens born while the collector runs, quoted in SOL. A wallet's profit comes from its own trades; the copier columns replay the real curve or pool to price what someone following it would have paid and received, landing one slot after the measured feed delay, with pump.fun's fee, the signature fee, a priority fee and a Jito tip on every transaction. A launcher's positions in its own tokens never count.

    

    

    

    
Followed live 

    
Return since each wallet was followed. The copy, solid: profit per SOL put into copies. The wallet itself, dashed: profit per SOL it spent on the tokens it bought since then, tokens it still holds at the live price. One colour per wallet, up to 8 at once, a point every 5 minutes. A golden wallet is followed for good; a top sniper only while it stays in the top, and a copy already open still exits on its sell.

How to read this
    
      
  • Golden means the copier made money in both halves of the window, the wallet itself was profitable in both halves, no two tokens make more than half of its winnings, it launches no tokens of its own, it is not part of a cluster, and the window spans at least 12 hours. Golden wallets are paper-followed live from that moment on: see Followed live, at the top.
    • 
      
  • Cluster ×N: N wallets bought the same tokens in the same slot on at least half of this wallet's tokens. That is one operator behind several wallets, or a coordinated group trading its own launches. Their edge is knowing the launch; it ends the moment they sell to their followers.
    • 
      
  • Same slot snipes land in the block that created the token: bundled with the launch or colocated with the validator. Nobody can copy those.
    • 
      
  • Operators group maker wallets that are probably one person. A maker that rotates to a fresh wallet every few launches still needs its own other wallets to snipe its launches, so the same early buyers turn up again; a buyer that appears for many different makers is a sniper bot doing its rounds and links nothing. Three shared snipers group two wallets. It is probable, never proven, and it is how a maker with five launches per wallet becomes a sample worth reading.
    • 
      
  • Coin makers are the one thing here you know about before it happens: a wallet that launches repeatedly will launch again, so it can be watched. Its row replays buying every launch two slots after creation and selling when the maker first sells, which is the honest version — the maker's own sell lands before yours, so you sell into its dump. A maker that never sells is not generous: your copy just times out at whatever the price is then.
    • 
      
  • What a copy pays. pump.fun's 1.25% on each side, plus 0.0015 SOL per transaction: the 0.000005 SOL signature fee, 0.0005 SOL of priority fee and a 0.001 SOL tip for a place at the top of the block. Twice, in and out, so about 3% of a 0.1 SOL copy before the price even moves. The priority fee and the tip are auctions, so those two are assumptions; raise them and most copying stops paying, which is the point of showing them.
    • 
      
  • The top snipers of the moment are paper-copied too, re-ranked every few minutes because that list turns over fast. They are ranked on tokens bought within two slots of creation over the last hours, so it is the busiest snipers, not the most profitable ones; the chart above is what tells you whether following them pays. Same-slot snipes are copied two slots late, because that is the earliest anyone could.
    • 
      
  • The top snipers of the moment are paper-copied as well, re-ranked every few minutes because that list turns over fast. It is ranked on tokens bought within two slots of creation over the last hours, so it is the busiest snipers, not the most profitable ones: the copy above is what tells you whether following them pays, and same-slot snipes are copied two slots late because that is the earliest anyone could.
    • 
      
  • Dev marks wallets that also launch tokens. A sniper that launches tokens is most likely buying its own or its friends' launches.
    • 
      
  • A token that graduates keeps being tracked on its PumpSwap pool, priced the same way, so positions and copies run straight through graduation.
    • 
      
  • Feed delay is measured against the chain's own slot clock. The copier lands one slot after it (at least two slots after the wallet): that is the best a copier on this feed could do, since a transaction lands in the next slot at the earliest. The live copies at the top measure what actually happens.

Strategy

      
Same launches, different ways in and out

      
Every launch by a wallet that launches repeatedly is bought the same way, then sold by a different rule, so the comparison is fair. The late60 and sol8 rules buy later instead, the way the steadiest wallet we follow does. If no rule beats copying the maker out, there is nothing here worth trading.

    

    

    
Which launches

    

    

    
Every rule on the same sample profit per launch, after the fee, the priority fee and the tip

    

    
How to read this
    
      
  • copy is the benchmark: out when the maker first sells, landing two slots behind it. The same rule the Pump tab replays.
    • 
      
  • breakeven takes the stake back the moment the position is worth it, then rides the rest out with the maker. If the maker sells first, it is simply the copy.
    • 
      
  • tp20 / tp50 / tp100 sell everything at that profit, counted after both transactions. If the price never gets there, they leave with the maker.
    • 
      
  • sell30s / sell60s leave on the clock, 30 or 60 seconds in, without waiting for the maker. Makers here sell their own bag after about a minute, so these test getting out ahead of that.
    • 
      
  • late60 / sol8 test the rhythm of 7VsGe3…, the steadiest wallet we follow: it buys a minute or more after launch, with about 8 SOL already in the curve, and holds about 2 minutes. late60_2m buys at 60 s and sells 2 minutes later; late60_tp20 / tp50 take +20% / +50% if that comes within those 2 minutes; late60_2m_held only takes coins whose maker had not sold by 60 s, something you know at that moment; sol8_2m buys once 8 SOL is in the curve instead. Each buys its own stake at its own, later price, landing two slots after the moment it decides.
    • 
      
  • hold has no rule at all: out at the end of the window. It is the control — a rule that cannot beat holding is noise.
    • 
      
  • Which launches. "Crew, second coin on" is the plan of watching an operator's crew: a crew we had already recognised sniped this launch, and the wallet had launched before, so the crew's move to it was already confirmed. "Crew, first coin" is that confirmation launch itself — the one you would skip. Everything is walked forward in time, so a crew counts only from what was known before the launch happened, never from what we learned later.
    • 
      
  • Every exit lands two slots after the trade that triggers it, because a rule can only react to a price it has already seen. Your own order would move the price too; that is not modelled, so every number here is a little kind.
    • 
      
  • 1st half / 2nd half split the window in two. A rule that only works in one half is a coincidence, not a strategy.
    • 
      
  • The sample is every launch by a wallet with several launches in the window. It says nothing about launches by one-time creators.
    • 
    

    
Mature coins bought once a coin has shown something, held minutes to hours

    
Bought when

    
Which coins

    

    

    

    
Wallets that buy past $100k what they made on coins they first bought at $100k or more, and what copying them would have made

    

    

    
How to read the mature coins
    
      
  • Wallets that buy past $100k ranks wallets by the coins they first bought at $100k of market cap or more, not coins they sniped and added to later. Own is what the wallet made itself, counting what it still holds at the last price. Copy replays 0.5 SOL bought two slots after its first buy and sold two slots after its first sell. A wallet passes when it made money in both halves of the window, a copy did too, and its two best coins are under half of its gains. Only coins born in the last three days are in the trade window, so older coins are out of sight.
    • 
      
  • Curve ~80% full buys once the coin is worth 300 SOL, about 80% of the way up its curve: the way 5K3N1v… buys. Just migrated buys the move to PumpSwap, reacting to the pool's first trade. Reached ~$100k buys the first time the coin is worth 870 SOL, about $100k at SOL $115. Still up 1 h after migrating buys an hour after the migration, if the coin still trades at or above its migration price.
    • 
      
  • Each entry is sold on the clock (1, 2, 5 or 30 min, 2 h, 6 h) or at a take-profit / stop-loss pair: tp20_sl10 sells at +20% or −10%, whichever comes first, else at 6 h; the same for +50 / −25 and +100 / −50.
    • 
      
  • Each trade is 0.5 SOL: into a pool of about 85 SOL, that keeps both the price impact and the 0.0015 SOL per transaction under 1%. On the pool a buy pays the fee the coin's sells pay, because PumpSwap's buy events log almost none.
    • 
      
  • Instant coins got there within a minute of launch, as far as you can know when buying: for "Curve ~80% full", the coin reached 300 SOL in its first minute (whether it then migrates is still to come); for the others, it had already migrated in its first minute, usually a bundle buying its own curve out. Organic is the rest.
    • 
      
  • A coin is looked at once, 30 hours after launch: anything it did in its first day can be an entry, and every exit falls inside the 6 hours after that, so no result is cut short. No entry into a pool under 20 SOL. Market cap is the price times a billion tokens.
